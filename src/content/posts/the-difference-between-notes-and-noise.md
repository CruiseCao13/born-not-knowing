Collecting information feels productive because it creates visible volume. Saved articles, screenshots, quotes, highlights, and links can look like progress.

But notes become noise when they are never processed, questioned, connected, or reviewed. A pile of information is not the same as understanding.

A useful note should do at least one thing: clarify a question, preserve evidence, record a mistake, explain a decision, connect ideas, or make a future review possible.

The test is simple: when I return to the note later, does it help me think? If not, it may only be storage.
